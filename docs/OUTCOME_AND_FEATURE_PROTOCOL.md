# Outcome and feature-availability protocol

The target `CVDSTRK3` asks whether a health professional ever told the respondent they had a stroke. Code 1 is positive and 2 negative; all other answers are excluded. Current predictor measurements and lifetime history do not identify future stroke incidence, acute severity, treatment response or causal risk factors.

The 15 inherited predictors are retained to make the study reproducible against the audited project. They are not an exhaustive clinically selected set. Sex, age, general health, exercise, smoking, diabetes, heart attack, coronary disease, kidney disease, arthritis, education and income are categorical; physically/mentally unhealthy days and BMI are numeric. Survey state, strata, PSU and final weights are never predictors. Natural predictor nonresponse is retained.

2024 and 2025 official codebooks were checked for all variables. Age code 14, smoker code 9, education code 9, income codes 77/99 and ordinary categorical 7/9 codes are missing. Income 7 and 9 are legitimate categories. Unhealthy-day code 88 means zero; 77/99 are missing. BMI has two implied decimal places; missing values and legacy 0/9999 handling follow the existing cleaner, whose defensive rules are documented in the data provenance audit. The 2025 codebook's BMI notes explicitly use 9999 for missing computed categories; this is not an inference that all extreme valid values are physiologically impossible.

The availability masks describe simulated data access, not actual clinical workflows or hospital resources. They operate at original-variable level and do not turn imputed values into observed measurements. Natural nonresponse differs from a deliberately unavailable column: the former is encoded by the fitted imputation/missing-category pipeline, the latter zeros the feature's entire encoded block and changes its availability flag.

Four masks are predefined: full (15 variables); without current-health-state variables (exclude GENHLTH, PHYSHLTH, MENTHLTH; 12 variables); demographics plus five disease-history items (7 variables); sex and age (2 variables). No ordering between the 12- and 7-variable sets is implied. Removing current-health-state questions is a sensitivity analysis, not proof that the remaining predictors predate stroke.

Full-model global calibration, full-model scenario calibration, mixed-mask training and subset retraining are separated. Calibration uses only the dedicated 2024 calibration partition; there is no 2025 adaptation. A model trained only on full inputs cannot learn the availability-flag effects because those flags are constant during fitting; this limitation is deliberately exposed by the fixed-model masking condition.

The primary contrast and inference are specified in the frozen JSON. Other family/scenario/group comparisons are secondary and descriptive. Group comparisons cover recorded sex, coarse age and income; they are not a comprehensive fairness assessment and do not include race/ethnicity. Survey-weighted metrics concern included reporting jurisdictions and respondents with known stroke-history labels; they are not a national clinical-risk estimate.
